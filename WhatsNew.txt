== 8.9.7 ==

New
- A log you delete is kept: its QSOs are written to an ADIF file first, so it can be brought back.
- Deleted Logs, in the Log Manager, opens that folder. Import Deleted Log brings a log back.
- Backups & Restore can move that folder, and the files already saved move with it.
- Notes: the ADIF note a QSO was imported with now has a column of its own.
- It is in the log table, in Full View & Edit, and in the Log Workshop, where you can search it.
- Name and Comment can be sorted by.
- Cluster: drag the Live Scale list to tune the radio.
- Cluster: a new-country spot is announced out loud.
- Cluster: every button, tick and column now says what it is when you hover it.
- The Msg buttons arrive named: CQ, Exch, TU and Call on CW, Msg1-Msg4 on voice.
- The CQ button can be named for the question it asks, and Enter works in every mode.
- Export to ADIF, Cabrillo or CSV opens on a folder of its own and remembers where you put it.
- The Try Again key shows how many are waiting on its face.
- My Locator fills itself from QRZ.com once the station callsign is set.
- Each log has its own upload queue.
- LoTW: the program finds TQSL by itself.
- The Log Workshop can search by what was uploaded.
- Radio Control Panel: a tick per band, an RTTY column, three more bands, RTTY, AM and FM.
- The CW keyer opens on any mode and Ctrl+K keeps it open.
- The macro editor holds both halves of a button: its name and its text.
- Grid locators of 10 characters are accepted and checked against the country.
- The log shows the QSO time with seconds.
- Import ADIF is one flow with fewer windows, and you decide every change.
- The Options page list is striped like the log table.

Fixed
- Full View & Edit: Contest ID, County and Credit Granted were thrown away on Save.
- Re-importing a file now fills in notes, and Undo no longer empties the carried ADIF.
- The Log Workshop opened too narrow: the end of a filter row was off the right edge.
- "Received Confirmation" was cut off top and bottom.
- Backspace could take back a letter the radio already had, and the keyer went deaf.
- Clear stopped promising F9 while the keyer owns it.
- The DX callsign is never framed in red, and Update fits its own name.
- Contest: the exchange cells fit inside their coloured bars.
- Cabrillo times are back to HHMM.
- Cluster: a plain arrow over the column headings, not a hand.

Faster
- The start no longer freezes for 13 seconds: a one-time repair of old notes ran on every start.
- Deleting a log, and exporting one, no longer read every QSO in the database.
- The Log Workshop ticks rows quickly again.

== 8.9.6 ==

New

- Three more fields in the QSO editor: Contest ID, County and Credit Granted.
- Right-click a QSO in the log and choose Edit and you get that same full window,
  wherever you ask from. Double-click still fills the form at the top of the window,
  in yellow, as before.

Fixed
- The red "unexpected error" box after ticking a box in a table is gone. It came from
  Windows itself, nothing was lost, and there was nothing you could do about it.
- Log Fixer: Check with AI is hidden rather than greyed out where it cannot be used.

== 8.9.5 ==

Fixed
- Your log comes back by itself. Updating from an older version could delete the log
  file: the old installer put a file of that name in your data folder, so Windows
  removed it as its own when it took the old version away. HolyLogger now notices and
  puts your log back from the newest daily backup before it finishes starting. You are
  not asked anything.
- And you are never asked to create a log again. If you have been using HolyLogger,
  the program opens your log. The create-a-log window is only for a brand new operator,
  or for a log from the very old single-log HolyLogger.
- If you already took 8.9.4, were asked, and made an empty log, your QSOs come back on
  this update.

New
- The update question is two lines instead of a page. It says the new version, your
  version, and asks. A "Show me what is new" button opens the list of changes, and
  closing it brings the question back.
- Cluster: the gear is out of the title bar. It now sits beside the undo icon, where it
  can be seen, and the window cannot be made narrow enough to hide it.
- Lite CW keyer on the menu bar: one line to type CW into without opening the CW Keyer
  window. It is there while that window is closed, with its own WPM and mouse wheel.
- The CQ button asks first. On a frequency you have not called on it sends QRL?, and the
  next press calls CQ. It sits red while it will ask. Right-click it to edit both texts.

== 8.9.4 ==

New
- CW keyer: twelve buttons in two banks of six, pressed by F1 to F12, with your own
  names on the keycaps.
- One editor for every CW text, opened from the keyer, with a standard set installed
  to start from.
- The CQ button asks first. On a frequency you have not called on it sends QRL?, and
  the next press calls CQ. It sits red while it will ask. This works on the keyer's
  first button and on the first of the four Msg buttons.
- WPM in the keyer's title bar, read from the radio itself. Roll the mouse wheel over
  it to change the speed.
- Lite CW keyer on the menu bar: one line to type CW into without opening the keyer
  window. It is there while that window is closed, and it has its own WPM and wheel.
- Enter Sends Message: Off, Run and S&P on the keyer's title bar, and a choice of
  typing straight onto the air or holding the line for Enter.
- The keyer's typing line no longer runs off the side. What the radio has finished
  with drops into the record below it.
- Cluster alerts: a watch list of callsigns. A spot on the list rings, is pinned to
  the top of the list and wears a purple frame.
- Right-click a spot and choose Copy to Alert to put that callsign on the list.
- The alerts have a sound of their own.
- A callsign that is not shaped like a callsign is now warned about, and can still be
  logged.
- Help > Which radios can send CW now covers the voice messages too. Every line was
  read from the maker's own manual.
- Log Fixer writes down what it changed, and can put it back.
- Activity list: Contest is a yellow key with a line under it, and Activate & Open Log
  is a green button.
- Tooltips: eight words to a line, and one colour in every scheme.

Fixed
- A UDP port could go deaf after a single failure and stay deaf until the program was
  restarted.
- Compact asks again when the FILE has grown, not only when the empty space has. A log
  declined once was never asked about again, however big it got.
- A message window could be measured wrongly on a scaled screen, so the text did not
  fit the window it was given.

Faster
- Startup: the LoTW list and the UDP sockets no longer hold up the window, and the
  splash gives back about three quarters of a second.
- The CW keyer redrew its typing line twenty times a second with nothing being sent.
  It redraws only when something has moved.

- QSOs you had just settled came straight back on the next check.

Faster

== 8.9.2 ==

New
- Radio Control Panel: a window of band buttons, each holding an SSB and a CW frequency, with
  SSB and CW buttons and a box to type a frequency into. Switched on in Options > User
  Interface.
- Tune with the mouse wheel: over the LED, over the panel's frequency box, or over the Live
  Scale spot list. A notch is worth what the pointer is over - kHz on the left of the point,
  Hz on the right - and the first notch tidies an odd frequency up to the whole kHz. It will
  not walk you outside the band.
- Tools > Open OmniRig opens OmniRig's own settings window, where the radio and its COM port
  are chosen. Until now that window could only be reached from OmniRig's tray icon.
- CW on a Yaesu: the keyer now works on the FT-891, FT-991, FT-991A, FTDX10, FTDX101D,
  FTDX101MP and FT-710. Yaesu has no command that keys typed text, so HolyLogger writes your
  text into CW memory 5 and plays it - keep nothing you want to save in memory 5.
- CW on any Icom: the radio's address is read from OmniRig's own file instead of a list of
  three, so every Icom OmniRig supports can be keyed - the IC-9700, IC-705 and IC-7100 among
  them.
- Help > Which radios can send CW: which radios HolyLogger can key, and whether yours is one.
  Every model on it was read out of its own maker's command manual.
- Messages tell you what to do: an error now says where things stand and the one thing worth
  trying, instead of only what went wrong. Where nothing can honestly be advised it says so
  rather than telling you to try again.
- Country box: the country on the main form is now a list. Type and it offers the countries
  whose name BEGINS with what you typed - digits find the entity number, so 33 finds 336.
  Picking one sets the name, the flag, the number, the continent and the zones together.
- Your own country wins: a country you choose by hand survives editing the callsign
  afterwards, and is what gets logged. Add or Clear ends it.
- Log Fixer remembers: a QSO it has put right is not raised again the next time you open it.
- Fixed filter: the Log Workshop can show only the QSOs the Log Fixer has corrected, or only
  the ones it has not.
- Search and Clear stay grey until a filter is set, and turn blue together. Escape does what
  Clear does, including nothing.
- Ask an AI: right-click a QSO and choose Ask AI to check this QSO. It reads the fields and
  writes back what it makes of them.
- Check with AI: in the Log Fixer, the QSOs where cty.dat and Club Log disagree can be put to
  an AI. It never changes anything - the line it believes turns green, the other turns grey.
- The AI must name its evidence: the prefix rule or the Club Log dates it used. The report
  keeps what it said, why, and which model said it.
- Options > AI Service: set it up in one place - credit, key and model - with a picture for
  every step. It is paid, on your own OpenRouter account: prepaid, a few cents a run, and no
  card is kept.
- CW Keyer: the keycaps are cyan, and View > CW Keyer opens it.
- Help > Visit HolyLogger site.
- What's New: every item starts with a bold subject, so the eye finds the one it wants.
- Send CW by typing: put the radio in CW and the CW Keyer opens by itself. What you type goes
  out as you type it. Ctrl+K opens and closes it at any time.
- Two rows: what is still to be sent on top, what has already gone below it in blue. The text
  turns blue letter by letter as the radio keys it.
- One line per send: each thing you send becomes its own line, pushing the earlier lines down.
- Typing too slowly: the radio falls silent in the middle of a word, which the listener hears
  as two words. When that happens a space is written into the line so you can see it.
- Set the keying speed from the keyer: the CW Keyer's title bar shows WPM and a number. Roll
  the mouse wheel over it and the radio's keyer speed changes with it, 5 to 30 words a minute,
  so you never reach past the program to the radio's own menu. Arrows beside the number are
  the hint that it turns.
- The speed is sent again when the keyer opens, so the number you are reading is the speed the
  radio is keying at. Turning the radio's own speed knob afterwards still wins - it is one
  setting with two controls, and the last one touched decides.
- Every speed command was taken from the maker's own command manual: KS on Kenwood, Yaesu and
  Elecraft, command 14 sub-command 0C on Icom, and each radio is held to the range its manual
  gives - a TS-480 no slower than 10, an Elecraft no slower than 8, an Icom no faster than 48.
- On a radio HolyLogger cannot command, the title bar still shows the speed measured from your
  keying, as before.
- Eight buttons: along the bottom, for the things you send over and over. Left-click sends,
  right-click edits. The first four are the same texts as the Msg buttons.
- Short marks: filled in when you send - * your Station Callsign, ! the DX Callsign, # the
  serial number, $ the rest of your sent exchange. The last two need contest mode. The editor
  shows a second row with exactly what will go on air.
- The keyer remembers: where you put it and how wide you made it.
- Try Again: keep the stations you meant to come back to, until you work them.
- Log Workshop: a window of its own.
- Undo: an edit in the main window can be taken back.
- Verify: checks a QSO and writes down what it found, as a table.
- Cluster: A, K and SFI bars.
- File > Compact the Database: gives back the space deleted QSOs leave behind.
- Statistics: answers "worked, but not confirmed".
- Splash screen: a spinner and the seconds.
- Sun and propagation: the figures now come from IARC.

Fixed
- "You changed settings since the profile was saved" was asked on every close, of operators who
  had changed nothing. The profile was watching things the program writes by itself - the
  radio's frequency above all, which moves every time you turn the VFO - and they are out of
  profiles now. They are still remembered from one session to the next; they simply no longer
  count as something you chose.
- A port typed in Options was forgotten if you closed the window with the X while the caret was
  still in the box. It is kept now, along with anything else typed on that page.
- CW keying refused radios it could key: an IC-7300-DATA is an IC-7300, and every Elecraft was
  turned away because of the name OmniRig gives it. K3, K3S, K4, KX2 and KX3 all key now.
- CW text was sent four characters too long for a Kenwood or an Elecraft, and the overflow was
  cut without a word.
- The CW keyer accepted pasted text a keyer cannot send - Hebrew, curly quotes - and then
  dropped every character of it on the way to the radio. It is cleaned as it is pasted now.
- A failed Add no longer empties the form. It used to tell you the QSO was not saved and wipe
  what you had typed in the same breath.
- Message windows grow to fit what they have to say, and a long one can no longer push its own
  OK button off the bottom of the screen.
- The log table now shows a correction the moment the Log Fixer writes it. A country put right
  appeared in the Log Workshop and stayed wrong in the main window behind it.
- Ticking the Fix box in the table header was a coffee break on a big log. It is immediate.
- Clearing the Workshop left the Country box grey instead of white.
- Custom image: choosing it no longer throws the file explorer open by itself.
- Options: the Browse button was too short for its own text.
- CW: the program worked out the length of a Morse message about a seventh too long, so the
  keying speed it showed was too fast and the moving cursor reached the last letter after you
  had already heard it. Both are fixed, on the Msg buttons as well.
- What's New: shown on the first run after every install, whatever the version number.
- Windows: one that is not in the taskbar can no longer be minimised out of sight.
- Import: Stop really stops, and undoes what it had done.
- Import: one report instead of several.
- Import: replacing a log can no longer lose it.
- Import: the list of skipped repeats is capped, and the count is right.
- Import: the spinner no longer stutters.
- Callsign list: it can no longer shrink by itself.
- Prefixes: typing SV1 is no longer treated as an unsaved QSO.
- OmniRig: you are now told when radio control cannot start.
- Remove Duplicates: you are told if a QSO could not be removed.
- Backups: capped by size, so they cannot fill the disk.
- Maps: no longer need the internet for their own program files.
- The update question: says the program will close and will not start again by itself, and
  shows your version beside the new one.

Faster
- Startup pause: the seven to eight seconds a moment after the window opens are gone.
- Opening a log: 28,000 QSOs in under two seconds instead of five and a half.
- The main window: appears in about half the time.
- Idle: much less processor used while you leave the program alone.
- Radio polling: no longer twice a second.
- Scrolling the log: lighter.
